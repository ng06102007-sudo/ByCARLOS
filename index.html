    The project

    A short film made for Spanish class, entirely in Spanish, about the 11 March 2004 Madrid train bombings (11-M), the deadliest terrorist attack in Spain's history.

    To tell such a heavy story in a way that speaks to students, we turned ourselves into LEGO minifigures. Each member of the group presents one part of the events against archive footage: the morning commute, the explosions on the trains, the government first blaming ETA, and a country that took to the streets to know the truth.

    The cast

    One minifigure per member of the group

    Process

    01Characters — four custom minifigures, rigged in Blender with the MecaFig add-on, with hand-made faces and expressions for the dialogue.
    02Sets — a Madrid commuter train carriage, the city and a sky of cartoon clouds, all built from bricks.
    03Animation — characters, camera moves and lighting, rendered in 4K.
    04Voice & edit — Spanish voice-over recorded by the group, archive footage and kinetic titles, edited in Premiere Pro.

      The project

      Storm Watch makes watches that join precision and bold design. Inspired by the force of nature, each piece looks for the balance between modern technology and a timeless look: a statement of style, toughness and innovation.

      I worked on the whole development of the watch. I took on its design, from the first research into its shape and how it works to setting its look and technical details. I made all the 3D models and renders, including the materials, textures and finishes, in Blender, with retouching in Photoshop and Lightroom. I also made the two versions of the model, in steel and in forged carbon, keeping the design and the brand's identity the same across both.

      The 3D model above is the real SW-02 assembly, taken from my Blender scenes: every part, from the case to the Miyota movement, the gold rotor and the screws. It comes in both versions, forged carbon and steel. Its hands show your time, and its movement runs at its real pace: the balance swings four times a second, the escape wheel ticks eight times a second, the seconds wheel turns once a minute.

      The watch · Steel Series

      01Crystal — scratch-resistant sapphire with an anti-reflective treatment, for exceptional clarity.
      02Hands — polished steel with a glossy black finish. The seconds hand carries the brand's signature.
      03Automatic movement — a Miyota 9039: 24 jewels, 28,800 vibrations per hour (4 Hz), a three-arm balance and a shock-protection system, for precision, reliability and lasting performance.
      04316L steel bezel — mirror-polished and brushed, a balance between brilliance and restraint.
      05316L steel case — monobloc, mirror-polished: strength, water resistance and precise assembly. Made in France.
      06Crown — screw-down and knurled, in mirror-polished steel with the brand's logo machined in, for a precise, secure grip.

      Sketches

      Pencil · front, side and back

      Process

      01Draw — the case, the bezel and the dial in pencil, from several angles.
      02Model — every part modelled as a real assembly (case, bezel, crown, dial, hands, crystals, gaskets, screws) around a Miyota 9039 movement, then brought into Blender. Grey exploded views check the build before anything gets a material.
      03Dress — materials, textures and finishes in Blender: forged carbon, brushed and polished steel, black PVD titanium, sapphire, rubber. Then retouching in Photoshop and Lightroom.
      04Versions — a forged-carbon SW-02 and a 316L Steel Series, each with orange, black, blue and green straps.
      05Campaign — posters and posts for social media: SW-01, SW-02 (“Hand made carbon watch, limited edition of 50 pieces”), the Steel Series launch, a Kickstarter teaser and the films.

      GAMAR

      GAMAR is a watch brand I created from scratch: the name, the emblem and the watches.

      Its emblem is a monogram of letters drawn as closed, interlocking loops, like the links of a bracelet, and it is engraved on the crown of every watch. The watches follow the same idea: sporty pieces with an architectural case, in steel or forged carbon, built around Swiss Sellita movements, with every detail designed down to the screws.

      Scroll

      01 / The brandSouth of France · Independent workshop

      GAMARMade to stand out.
      Made to be worn, every day.

      GAMAR is an independent watch brand from the South of France. Each watch is drawn as one architecture: the case, the movement and the dial are developed together, so that no part looks added at the end.

      02 / Material

      Two materials.
      One architecture.

      Every design is expressed in two materials with opposite characters. Stainless steel gives a sharp, brushed and polished geometry. Forged carbon, chopped fibres pressed under heat, never forms the same pattern twice: every carbon case carries a surface of its own.

      • DesignSouth of France
      • MaterialsSteel · forged carbon
      • ProductionSmall batches
      • ApproachIndependent

      03 / The watchmakerMachining · assembly · regulation

      Justin
      Martorell.

      Justin Martorell is the watchmaker GAMAR works with. I modelled and designed the GAMAR watches with him, in 3D, part by part; he brings them to life in his workshop: he machines the cases, assembles the movements, regulates and checks every watch.

      He did not inherit a manufacture or a family name to carry on. He started from curiosity for mechanical objects, and chose to learn how a watch is actually made.

      Built, not inherited.

      04 / Before watches

      First,
      machines.

      Before working to the millimetre, he learned on a larger scale by restoring an old Mini. The method was the one he still uses on a watch: take it apart, understand it, rebuild it better. A car and a mechanical watch differ in size, but both live on proportions, tolerances and parts that move together.

      1. 01Disassemble
      2. 02Understand
      3. 03Rebuild

      05 / The making

      Machined by precision.
      Finished by hand.

      Each watch exists first as geometry: proportions, surfaces and the way every component meets the next are fixed before any material is cut. Then the forged carbon is CNC-machined in the workshop, step by step, down to the final dimensions of the case.

      When the machine stops, the hand takes over. Every part is handled, fitted and checked before a watch leaves the bench.

      1. 01Finishthe surfaces get their last touch
      2. 02Assemblecase, movement and parts become one
      3. 03Regulatethe movement is checked and adjusted
      4. 04Inspecta last visual and working control

      06 / The principle

      1. 01Understand
      2. 02Control
      3. 03Refine
      4. 04Build

      Understand the object, master the material, refine every detail, and keep building until it feels right. Curiosity first, understanding second, making last.

      The brief

      DNMADe year 1 · Graphic design · Sequence 1 · 3 to 21 September 2026

      Design the logo of Maison Laget. It will be printed on many supports, goodies and documents, so it has to carry the spirit of the business and the idea of terroir, with blue as the main colour and possibly red and touches of pink.

      01Investigation

      What makes a logo: shapes, colours and what they suggest, composition. Simplify a shape in three steps, define a monogram, take apart an existing logo, and build a word cloud around Maison Laget with its colours.

      02Experiments

      Three hands-on tries: a logo cut from natural elements such as leaves and flowers; gouache-painted paper cut straight with the scissors, as Matisse did, with newspaper and tracing paper; and outlines made with wool.

      03Proposals

      Three logos: two that recall terroir products, quality work and nature, and one free logo in two colours at most, presented on two annotated boards titled “Logotypes Maison Laget”. Then improve a vintage logo generated by AI for the shop.

      04Finish

      The chosen logo finalised in desktop publishing (PAO).

      The place

      Maison Laget, near the covered market in the centre of Langogne, Lozère

      Maison Laget is a small family butcher's, charcuterie and caterer in the centre of Langogne, in Lozère, near the covered market. Founded in 1987, it sells quality regional products and, attached to tradition, belongs to the town's brotherhood of Gargantua. Behind its stone front and its arches, there was no logo: only the words “Boucherie Charcuterie” in red letters across the stone.

      Kallhack

      Object design · Hacking the IKEA KALLAXSchool project

      The project board

      01TypeObject design · hacking
      02ObjectThe IKEA KALLAX shelf
      03First ideaKALLHACK, a kitchen island
      04Final projectKIDÄX, a modular unit for a child's bedroom
      05SystemMagnets and wooden dowels
      06ToolsPencil, markers, coloured pencils

      What is hacking?

      Hacking, in design, means taking an object that already exists and giving it a new function, a new look or a new meaning. It is a way to recycle, transform and question how we use everyday objects. Two references opened the project: Joe Scanlan's “Kläps”, a coffin built from an IKEA bookcase with its own assembly instructions, and Daniel Saakes, who turned the IKEA LAMPAN lamp into a new light.

      Sketchbook · 1 / 9

      The KALLAX

      The starting object is the KALLAX shelf: plain white, simple and sober. IKEA, world leader in furniture, designs it for every kind of customer: professionals, families who like how simple it is to build, students who like its low price. It measures 76.5 × 146.5 cm with 33.5 cm cubes and 39.5 cm depth, it is assembled with wooden dowels, and it can stand upright or lie flat.

      Sketchbook · 2 / 9

      First idea · KALLHACK

      The first hack turned the KALLAX into a central kitchen unit, a pass-through island with a sink and a tap. A black granite worktop is elegant, waterproof and strong, but heavy: steel rods run inside each upright to carry its weight, two KALLAX side by side make it longer, a wooden plinth or IKEA CAPITA steel legs bring it to a good working height, and a protective paint makes it stand grease and humidity.

      Sketchbook · 3 to 5 / 9

      The turn · KIDÄX

      The project then moved to a child's bedroom. The aim: a piece of furniture that is educational, practical, simple, modular and fun, with games built into it. A first version filled the cubes with a marble run, a teddy bear, a mirror, a screen and a chalkboard, but it did not meet the expectations: it had to become truly modular, with magnets, hooks and fixings.

      Sketchbook · 6 and 7 / 9

      The module

      A main structure receives shelves that the child places where they want: a magnet system lets each part fit anywhere on the unit, while wooden dowels lock the shelf firmly to the main block. The project lets children express their creativity by turning the KALLAX into a unit that adapts to each child and to what they want.

      Sketchbook · 8 / 9

      The final project

      KIDÄX in the bedroom, in two configurations, with its parts listed as in an IKEA manual: the panels, the dowels, the magnets and the screws.

      Dormir coûte que coûte

      Object design · A nap cushion for public transportSchool project

      The prototype · base plate, suction cups and cushion

      01TypeObject design
      02ObjectA cushion that sticks to the window of a bus or a train
      03ForCommuters who sleep on the way
      04Size17 × 11 cm, 6 cm thick when inflated
      05MaterialsStretch velvet · PVC · polypropylene
      06ToolsPencil, markers, coloured pencils · 3D printing · sewing

      The question

      How do you design an accessory that suspends the time of performance, for a pause that prepares you for what comes next?

      Today everything goes fast and we are often tired: rest is pushed aside for work, screens and obligations, though sleep is essential for the body and the mind. We leave early and come home late, so sleep gets harder and harder to find. That is where nomadic sleep comes in: sleeping away from your own bed, on the bus, on the train, during a break.

      Sketchbook · 1 / 5

      Métro, boulot, dodo

      With the noise, the crowd and the pace of transport, finding a real moment of rest on a train or a bus is hard. A reference: the FRAKTA bag cushion by IKEA and The Secret Little Agency (2024), a bag that turns into a pillow so you can rest on public transport. The user: a 17-year-old who rides public transport alone and comes back tired from work. What he asks for: comfort, light and easy to carry, made to sleep on the way, compact, sober, good for a micro-nap or a long one, and fixed to the window.

      Sketchbook · 2 / 5

      Two ideas

      The constraints: the window ledge, the vibrations, the cold glass, every kind of public transport, comfort and a small size. A first idea rests a cushion on a folding board placed on the window ledge. The second, the one kept, is an inflatable cushion on a thin board with mini suction cups: it sticks to the window or the wall, at the height of the head.

      Sketchbook · 3 / 5

      Size and materials

      17 × 11 cm and 6 cm thick once inflated, with five 4 cm suction cups. Stretch velvet for the cover, very soft, cocooning, it follows the shape of the face. PVC for the inflatable cushion, strong and insulating against the cold of the glass. Polypropylene for the base, light, strong, slightly flexible, it stands heat and water. PVC suction cups, easy to put on and take off, with no mark left.

      Sketchbook · 4 / 5

      On the way

      The curves of the cushion recall comfort, and so sleep. The stretch velvet gives it a soft, high-end feel, a sense of quality that makes the object personal and strengthens the link between it and the person using it.

                  iMac 2009

                  Upcycling · From computer to HDMI monitor2026

                  Step-by-step tutorial · 2:54 · shot in D-Log, graded

                  01TypeUpcycling · DIY electronics
                  02ObjectApple iMac 21.5″, late 2009
                  03ModelA1311 · iMac10,1
                  04PanelLG LM215WF3 (SL)(A1) · 1920 × 1080
                  05ControllerM.NT68676 · HDMI / DVI / VGA
                  06PowerExternal 12 V · 5 A · 60 W
                  07Filmed onDJI Osmo Pocket · D-Log

                  The project

                  A 2009 iMac had become too old to use as a computer. Instead of throwing it away, I kept what still works perfectly: its Full HD screen and its aluminium body.

                  I removed every computer part and installed a new controller board, so the LCD panel can be plugged straight into any modern computer over HDMI. An obsolete object gets a second life, without losing its design.

                  The kit

                  Controller board, LED backlight driver and LVDS cable

                  Process

                  01Identify — model A1311 and, above all, the panel reference: every LCD needs a controller made for it.
                  02Open — the glass is held by magnets, not glue: two suction cups lift it off intact.
                  03Extract the panel — eight Torx T10 screws, then four cables unplugged: vertical sync, backlight, LVDS video, temperature sensor.
                  04Empty the body — DVD drive, hard drive, fans, logic board, CPU cooling and old display electronics come out and are kept for reuse. The original power supply holds charged capacitors: removing it is a job for a qualified professional.
                  05Check the panel — the label on the back confirms LM215WF3 (SL)(A1), matching the controller kit.
                  06Wire the image — the kit's LVDS cable runs from the controller to the panel's 30-pin video socket, pin order checked first.
                  07Wire the light — a separate driver powers the LED backlight through a 6-pin connector, after checking the pinout matches.
                  08Power — an external 12 V brick replaces Apple's supply; the board makes the 5 V the panel needs.
                  09First test — outside the body: the board starts, the backlight turns on, the image shows at 1920 × 1080.
                  10Fit & close — boards on insulating stands away from the aluminium, cables kept clear, HDMI and buttons within reach; panel screwed back and glass snapped on.

                  Photos

                  What I learned

                  How an all-in-one computer is built inside, how an LCD panel works — one circuit for the image, another for the light — and how to fit new electronics into an existing object. A design approach based on repair and reuse: rather than replacing an object, change what it does.

                  Result21.5″ Full HD · HDMI
